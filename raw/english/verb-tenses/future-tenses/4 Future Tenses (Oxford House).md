# 4 Future Tenses In English And How To Use Them

## 1. Future Simple
Form: will/won't + base infinitive. Will is a modal verb, doesn't change with person. Contraction: I'll. Won't more common in speech than "will not" (used for emphasis).

Uses and examples:
- Instant/spontaneous decisions: "I'm hungry. I think I'll make a sandwich."
- Future predictions based on a belief: "I'm sure you'll pass the test."
- Promises: "I won't tell anyone your secret."
- Offers: "I'll carry your bags for you."
- Requests: "Will you tell Henry I called?"
- Threats: "If you do that again, I'll tell Mum."
- Future facts: "I'll be back later tonight."

### Shall
Can replace "will" for future time references with I/we, slightly more formal: "We shall never forget this beautiful day." Common in questions to make offers, suggestions, or ask for advice: "Shall I carry these bags for you?" / "Shall I open the window?" / "What shall I tell Mary about the broken vase?"

### Be going to vs will
For predictions based on evidence and for future plans, use "be going to" not "will": "Look at those grey clouds. It's definitely going to rain!" / "I'm going to the gym."

## 2. Future Continuous
Form: will/won't + be + -ing.

Uses and examples:
- Action in progress at a specific future time: "This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Barbados."
- Action seen as new or temporary: "I'll be working for my Dad until I find a new job."
- Predictions/guesses about future events: "He'll be coming to the party, I guess."
- Predictions about the present: "She'll be getting married right now, I imagine."
- Polite enquiries: "Will you be joining us for dinner?"

Stative verbs not used in continuous forms.

## 3. Future Perfect
Form: will/won't + have + past participle.

Uses and examples:
- Action completed before a specific future time: "Next September, we'll have been married for 50 years."
- "by" / "by the time" = some time before: "I'll have finished this report by the time you're home."
- "in" / "in a day's time" / "in two weeks' time" / "in three months' time" = at the end of this period: "In three years' time, I'll have completed my degree."

## 4. Future Perfect Continuous
Emphasizes duration of an action up to a future point (examples given via practice activity, e.g. "He'll have been teaching grammar for four hours").
