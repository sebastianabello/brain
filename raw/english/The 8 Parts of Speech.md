
A **part of speech** (also called a **word class**) is a category that describes the role a word plays in a sentence. Understanding the different parts of speech can help you analyze how words function in a sentence and improve your writing.

The parts of speech are classified differently in different grammars, but most traditional grammars list eight parts of speech in English: **[nouns](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#nouns)**, **[pronouns](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#pronouns)**, **[verbs](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#verbs)**, **[adjectives](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#adjectives)**, **[adverbs](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#adverbs)**, **[prepositions](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#prepositions)**, **[conjunctions](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#conjuctions)**, and **[interjections](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#interjections)**. Some modern grammars add others, such as **[determiners](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#determiners)** and **[articles](https://www.scribbr.com/category/parts-of-speech/#articles)**.

Many words can function as different parts of speech depending on how they are used. For example, “laugh” can be a **noun** (e.g., “I like your laugh”) or a **verb** (e.g., “don’t laugh”).

## Nouns

A [**noun**](https://www.scribbr.com/category/nouns-and-pronouns/) is a word that refers to a person, concept, place, or thing. Nouns can act as the subject of a sentence (i.e., the person or thing performing the action) or as the object of a verb (i.e., the person or thing affected by the action).

There are numerous types of nouns, including [**common nouns**](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/common-nouns/) (used to refer to nonspecific people, concepts, places, or things), [**proper nouns**](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/proper-nouns/) (used to refer to specific people, concepts, places, or things), and [**collective nouns**](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/collective-nouns/) (used to refer to a group of people or things).

Examples: Nouns in a sentence

I’ve never read that **book**.

**Ella** lives in **France**.

The **band** played only new **songs**.

Other types of nouns include [countable and uncountable nouns](https://www.scribbr.com/category/nouns-and-pronouns/#countable-uncountable), [concrete nouns](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/concrete-noun/), [abstract nouns](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/abstract-noun/), and [gerunds](https://www.scribbr.com/category/nouns-and-pronouns/#gerunds).

Note

**Proper nouns** (e.g., “New York”) are always capitalized. **Common nouns** (e.g., “city”) are only [capitalized](https://www.scribbr.com/language-rules/capitalization-rules/) when they’re used at the start of a sentence.

## Pronouns

A [**pronoun**](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/pronouns/) is a word used in place of a noun. Pronouns typically refer back to an antecedent (a previously mentioned noun) and must demonstrate correct [pronoun-antecedent agreement](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/pronoun-antecedent-agreement/). Like nouns, pronouns can refer to people, places, concepts, and things.

There are numerous types of pronouns, including [**personal pronouns**](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/personal-pronouns/) (used in place of the proper name of a person), [**demonstrative pronouns**](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/demonstrative-pronouns/) (used to refer to specific things and indicate their relative position), and [**interrogative pronouns**](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/pronouns/#interrogative) (used to introduce questions about things, people, and ownership).

Examples: Pronouns in a sentence

**I** don’t really know **her**.

**That** is a horrible painting!

**Who** owns the nice car?

## Verbs

A [**verb**](https://www.scribbr.com/language-rules/verbs/) is a word that describes an action (e.g., “jump”), occurrence (e.g., “become”), or state of being (e.g., “exist”). Verbs indicate what the subject of a sentence is doing. Every complete sentence must contain at least one verb.

Verbs can change form depending on **subject** (e.g., [first person](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/first-person-pronouns/) singular), **tense** (e.g., simple past), **mood** (e.g., interrogative), and **voice** (e.g., [passive voice](https://www.scribbr.com/academic-writing/passive-voice/)).

[**Regular verbs**](https://www.scribbr.com/language-rules/verbs/#regular-irregular) are verbs whose [**simple past**](https://www.scribbr.com/verbs/simple-past-tense/) and [**past participle**](https://www.scribbr.com/verbs/past-participles/) are formed by adding“-ed” to the end of the word (or “-d” if the word already ends in “e”). [**Irregular verbs**](https://www.scribbr.com/verbs/irregular-verbs/) are verbs whose simple past and past participles are formed in some other way.

Examples: Regular and irregular verbs

“Will you **check** if this book is in stock?”

“I’ve already **checked** twice.”

“I heard that you used to **sing**.”

“Yes! I **sang** in a choir for 10 years.”

Other types of verbs include [auxiliary verbs](https://www.scribbr.com/verbs/auxiliary-verb/), [linking verbs](https://www.scribbr.com/verbs/linking-verb/), [modal verbs](https://www.scribbr.com/language-rules/verbs/#modal), and [phrasal verbs](https://www.scribbr.com/language-rules/verbs/#phrasal).

## Adjectives

An [**adjective**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adjectives/) is a word that describes a noun or pronoun. Adjectives can be **attributive**, appearing before a noun (e.g., “a **red** hat”), or **predicative**, appearing after a noun with the use of a [linking verb](https://www.scribbr.com/language-rules/verbs/#linking) like “to be” (e.g., “the hat **is red**”).

Adjectives can also have a comparative function. [**Comparative adjectives**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adjectives/#comparative-superlative) compare two or more things. **Superlative adjectives** describe something as having the most or least of a specific characteristic.

Examples: Adjectives in a sentence

The dog is **bigger** than the cat.

He is the **laziest** person I know

Other types of adjectives include [coordinate adjectives](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adjectives/#coordinate), [participial adjectives](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adjectives/#participial), and [denominal adjectives](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adjectives/#denominal).

## Adverbs

An [**adverb**](https://www.scribbr.co.uk/the-parts-of-speech/adverb/) is a word that can modify a verb, adjective, adverb, or sentence. Adverbs are often formed by adding “-ly” to the end of an adjective (e.g., “slow” becomes “slowly”), although not all adverbs have this ending, and not all words with this ending are adverbs.

There are numerous types of adverbs, including [**adverbs of manner**](https://www.scribbr.co.uk/the-parts-of-speech/adverb/#manner) (used to describe how something occurs), [**adverbs of degree**](https://www.scribbr.co.uk/the-parts-of-speech/adverb/#degree) (used to indicate extent or degree), and [**adverbs of place**](https://www.scribbr.co.uk/the-parts-of-speech/adverb/#place) (used to describe the location of an action or event).

Examples: Adverbs in a sentence

Ray acted **rudely**.

Talia writes **quite** quickly.

Let’s go **outside**!

Other types of adverbs include [adverbs of frequency](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adverbs/#frequency), [adverbs of purpose](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adverbs/#purpose), [focusing adverbs](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/adverbs/#focusing), and [adverbial phrases](https://www.scribbr.co.uk/syntax/adverbial/).

## Prepositions

A [**preposition**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/prepositions/) is a word (e.g., “at”) or phrase (e.g., “on top of”) used to show the relationship between the different parts of a sentence. Prepositions can be used to indicate aspects such as **time**, **place**, and **direction**.

Examples: Prepositions in a sentence

Hasan is coming for dinner **at** 6 p.m.

I left the cup **on** the kitchen counter.

Carey walked **to** the shop.

Note

A single preposition can often describe many different relationships, depending upon how it’s used. For example, “in” can indicate time (“in January”), location (“in the garage”), purpose (“in reply”), and so on. ## Conjunctions

A [**conjunction**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/conjunctions/) is a word used to connect different parts of a sentence (e.g., words, phrases, or clauses).

The main types of conjunctions are [**coordinating conjunctions**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/conjunctions/#coordinating-conjunctions) (used to connect items that are grammatically equal), [**subordinating conjunctions**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/conjunctions/#subordinating-conjunctions) (used to introduce a dependent clause), and [**correlative conjunctions**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/conjunctions/#correlative-conjunctions) (used in pairs to join grammatically equal parts of a sentence).

Examples: Conjunctions in a sentence

Daria likes swimming **and** hiking.

You can choose what movie we watch **because** I chose the last time.

We can **either** go out for dinner **or** go to the [theater](https://www.scribbr.com/us-vs-uk/theatre-vs-theater/).

## Interjections

An [**interjection**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/interjections/) is a word or phrase used to express a feeling, give a command, or greet someone. Interjections are a grammatically independent part of speech, so they can often be excluded from a sentence without affecting the meaning.

Types of interjections include [**volitive interjections**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/interjections/#volitive) (used to make a demand or request), [**emotive interjections**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/interjections/#emotive) (used to express a feeling or reaction), [**cognitive interjections**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/interjections/#cognitive) (used to indicate thoughts), and [**greetings and parting words**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/interjections/#greetings) (used at the beginning and end of a conversation).

Examples: Interjections in a sentence

**Psst**. What time is it?

**Ouch**! I hurt my arm.

I’m, **um**, not sure.

**Hey**! How are you doing?

## Other parts of speech

The traditional classification of English words into eight parts of speech is by no means the only one or the objective truth. Grammarians have often divided them into more or fewer classes. Other commonly mentioned parts of speech include determiners and articles.

### Determiners

A [**determiner**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/determiners/) is a word that describes a noun by indicating quantity, possession, or relative position.

Common types of determiners include [**demonstrative determiners**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/determiners/#demonstrative) (used to indicate the relative position of a noun), [**possessive determiners**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/determiners/#possessive) (used to describe ownership), and [**quantifiers**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/determiners/#quantifiers) (used to indicate the quantity of a noun).

Examples: Determiners in a sentence

**This** chair is more comfortable than **that** one.

**My** brother is selling **his** old car.

**Many** friends of mine have part-time jobs.

Other types of determiners include [distributive determiners](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/determiners/#distributive), [determiners of difference](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/determiners/#difference), and [numbers](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/determiners/#numbers).

NoteIn the traditional eight parts of speech, these words are usually classed as adjectives, or in some cases as pronouns.

### Articles

An [**article**](https://www.scribbr.com/parts-of-speech/articles/) is a word that modifies a noun by indicating whether it is specific or general.

- The definite article **the** is used to refer to a specific version of a noun. **The** can be used with all [countable and uncountable nouns](https://www.scribbr.com/nouns-and-pronouns/uncountable-nouns/) (e.g., “the door,” “the energy,” “the mountains”).
- The indefinite articles **a** and **an** refer to general or unspecific nouns. The indefinite articles can only be used with singular countable nouns (e.g., “a poster,” “an engine”).

Examples: Definite and indefinite articles in a sentence

I live just outside of **the** town.

There’s **a** concert this weekend.

Karl made **an** offensive gesture.

Note

While articles are often considered their own part of speech, they are also frequently classed as a type of determiner (or, in some grammars, as a type of adjective).
