# Subject-Verb Agreement: Comprehensive Guide

## Definición y regla básica
La concordancia entre sujeto y verbo significa que ambos deben coincidir en número. Sujetos singulares usan verbos singulares; sujetos plurales usan verbos plurales.

## Ejemplos básicos
- Singular + singular: "She works every day", "My teacher explains grammar clearly", "The dog runs fast"
- Plural + plural: "The dogs run fast", "My friends study English", "They enjoy traveling"

## Casos especiales

**Con "and"**: dos sujetos unidos por "and" normalmente toman verbo plural: "Sarah and Mike are classmates." Excepción: "Macaroni and cheese is my favorite food" (concepto único).

**Con "or/nor"**: el verbo concuerda con el sujeto más cercano: "Either the students or the teacher is presenting today" vs. "Neither my friends nor my brother likes spicy food."

**Pronombres indefinidos**: "everyone," "somebody," "nobody" son gramaticalmente singulares: "Everyone loves music" (no "love"). Algunos varían según contexto: "Some of the cake is gone" vs. "Some of the students are absent."

**Sustantivos colectivos**: generalmente singulares en inglés americano: "The team is practicing today", "The family lives in Chicago."

**Frases intercaladas**: el sujeto real —no los sustantivos cercanos— determina la concordancia: "The box of books is heavy" (sujeto: "box", no "books").

## Errores comunes
- "She go to school" → "She goes to school"
- "Everyone have homework" → "Everyone has homework"
- "My brother and sister lives nearby" → "My brother and sister live nearby"
