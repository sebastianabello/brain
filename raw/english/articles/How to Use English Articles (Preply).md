# Guía Completa sobre Artículos en Inglés

An article is "a tiny word that precedes a noun and indicates whether it is singular or plural form, in specific instances or unspecific ones."

## Types
Three articles: A/An (indefinite), The (definite).

## Indefinite article (A/An)
General use — shows a noun refers to something general, not specific:
1. With singular countable nouns: "I want to buy a car"
2. First mention: introduces something new before using "the"
3. Professions: "He is a firefighter"

### A vs. An
Phonetic rule, not spelling: "a" before consonant sound, "an" before vowel sound. Exceptions: "an hour" (silent h), "a university" (u sounds like [ju:]), "an MBA" (M sounds like [em]).

### With adjectives
Depends on the adjective's initial sound, not the noun's: "a generous man" but "an interesting book".

### Limitations
Not used with plural nouns or uncountable nouns (water, advice, information).

## Definite article (The)
When to use:
1. Specific reference: "She is the doctor I came to see"
2. Previous mention: after introducing with a/an
3. Uniqueness: "The moon is huge tonight"
4. Superlatives and ordinals: "the most interesting movie"
5. General groups (species): "The tiger is a dangerous animal"

### With proper nouns
Used with: countries with words like "state" or "kingdom" ("the United States"), plural countries ("the Philippines"), geographical features ("the Rocky Mountains", "the Atlantic"), famous buildings ("the Colosseum Theatre"), newspapers ("The Sun, The Times"), works of art ("the Mona Lisa"), organizations ("the United Nations"), hotels and restaurants ("the Plaza, the Hilton").

Not used with: individual people's names, simple country names ("Germany", "Berlin"), languages, months, some specific locations ("Big Ben", "Stonehenge").

## Zero article
Omitted with: proper nouns, uncountable nouns in general sense ("Clean air is vital"), indefinite plural nouns ("Children like chocolates").

## Uncountable nouns
Include liquids, bulk foods, abstract concepts, feelings, natural phenomena, materials, and collective nouns. Use quantity expressions: "a cup of water", "some coffee".

Some nouns function both ways depending on context: "I went to London four times" (countable) vs. "I didn't have time" (uncountable).

## Summary table

| Previous mention | the | "The book is fascinating" |
| Uncountable noun | none | "advice", "water" |
| Uniqueness | the | "the moon" |
| Profession | a/an | "a doctor" |
