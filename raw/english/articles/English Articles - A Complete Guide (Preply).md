# Guía Exhaustiva sobre Artículos en Inglés

English has three articles: one definite (the) and two indefinite (a, an). "Articles always come before nouns (or adjectives + nouns)."

## A vs. An
Depends on sound, not spelling.
- "A" before consonant sounds: "a book," "a pencil," "a university" (u → "yu"), "a house" (h pronounced)
- "An" before vowel sounds: "an apple," "an elephant," "an hour" (silent h), "an honest person," "an MBA" (acronyms starting with vowel sound)

## The vs. A/An
- The: specific references known to speaker and listener; previously mentioned items; unique entities ("the sun," "the moon"); with superlatives ("the best," "the most important")
- A/An: first mentions ("I bought a new phone"); general examples; professions ("She is a doctor"); rates/frequency ("twice a day")

## Zero article
Omitted with: languages ("I study English"), most meals ("Let's have lunch"), abstract concepts ("Love is important"), general plural nouns ("Dogs are loyal"), countries and cities generally ("France is beautiful")

## Uncountable nouns
Never take "a"/"an": with specificity ("the water in this bottle"), generically ("water is essential"), with quantifying phrases ("a piece of information," "a bottle of water")

## Articles with adjectives
The article precedes the adjective and is chosen based on the adjective's initial sound: "a blue book" (consonant), "an interesting story" (vowel), "the old car" (specific)

## Articles and possessive pronouns
Never combine articles with possessives (my, your, his, her, their): "my book" (correct), not "the my book" or "a your car" — the possessive replaces the article.

## Common errors
1. Wrong sound: "a elephant" instead of "an elephant"
2. Unnecessary articles: "The life is beautiful" should be "Life is beautiful"
3. Omitted articles: "I am student" should include "a"
4. Articles with uncountables: "a water" is incorrect; should be "water" or "a glass of water"
