# The 8 Parts of Speech

Parts of speech are the categories into which words are classified based on their functions in a sentence.

## 1. Nouns
Name people, places, concepts, or objects.
- Common vs. Proper: common are generic ("planet"), proper designate specific names ("Jupiter")
- Concrete vs. Abstract: concrete refer to physical objects ("flowers"), abstract to intangible qualities ("love")

## 2. Pronouns
Replace already-known nouns.
- Subjective: I, he, she, we, it, you, they
- Objective: me, him, her, us, them
- Possessive: mine, yours, theirs, ours, his, hers
- Demonstrative: this, that, these, those

Note: the singular neutral pronoun "they/them/their" is increasingly common as a gender-neutral pronoun.

## 3. Adjectives
Describe nouns.
- Descriptive: red, large, cheerful
- Quantitative: many, three, few
- Comparative: taller, more beautiful
- Superlative: tallest, most beautiful

Adjective order rule: Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose
Example: "lovely small old round brown British wooden coffee table"

## 4. Verbs
Describe physical or mental actions.
- Action vs. non-action: "runs" is action; "seems" expresses state
- Transitive vs. intransitive: transitive verbs require a direct object ("closed the door"); intransitive do not ("sleeps")

## 5. Adverbs
Modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.
- Time: yesterday, always, soon
- Place: here, outside, everywhere
- Manner: quietly, quickly, gracefully
- Degree: very, extremely, almost

Common indicator: "-ly" ending, though not all adverbs have it.

## 6. Prepositions
Indicate relationships between words.
- Direction: to, into, toward
- Location: in, on, under
- Time: at, before, during
- Cause/Reason: because of, due to

## 7. Conjunctions
Link complex ideas. Three types:
- Coordinating: and, but, or, yet
- Subordinating: because, although, if
- Correlative: either/or, neither/nor

## 8. Interjections
Express strong emotions or sudden reactions. Examples: Wow!, Oops!, Hooray! Recommended to avoid in formal writing.

## Multi-function words
Many words function as different parts of speech depending on context:
- Work: noun ("I went to work") / verb ("I work in the garden")
- Well: adverb ("paints very well") / adjective ("are finally well") / noun ("dropped penny into the well")
- But: conjunction / preposition ("everything but the pens")
- Like: verb / preposition / conjunction

## Open vs. closed word classes
- Open (acquire new words): nouns, adjectives, adverbs, verbs. Example: "adulting" (verb, 2010s)
- Closed (fixed): pronouns, conjunctions, prepositions

## Identification tips
1. Adjective + "-ly" = adverb ("commonly, quickly")
2. Interchangeable with a noun = pronoun
3. Modifiable with "do" = verb ("do have")
4. Removable without essential loss = adjective ("red van")
