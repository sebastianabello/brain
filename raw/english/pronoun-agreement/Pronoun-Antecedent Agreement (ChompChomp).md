# Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

A pronoun must agree with its antecedent. A singular antecedent requires a singular pronoun; a plural antecedent needs a plural pronoun.

## Pronoun forms
- Singular: he, she, it; him, her; his, her, hers, its; himself, herself, itself
- Plural: they; them; their, theirs; themselves

## Special cases

### Each and every
Even when two nouns are joined by "and," "each" or "every" preceding them forces singular agreement:
> Each butterfly and bee drank its fill.

### Correlative conjunctions
With "not only...but also" constructions, agreement follows the closer of the two antecedents.

### Singular indefinite pronouns
Each, either, neither, one, anybody, everyone, nobody, someone are technically singular, though modern usage has evolved:
- Past: exclusively masculine ("everyone returned to his spot")
- Intermediate: "his or her" (considered awkward)
- Contemporary: plural "they," or rephrasing with a plural noun

### Variable-number indefinite pronouns
"All, any, none, some, most, more" take their number from the noun in the following prepositional phrase:
> Some of this footwear smells... it needs to be thrown out.
> Some of these shoes smell... they need to be thrown out.

### Collective nouns
Context-dependent:
- Unified action: "The family is enjoying its dinner."
- Individual actions: "The family members are eating their meals separately."

Simplifying solutions: substitute a regular plural, or add "members."

### Businesses, schools, organizations
Always singular: "Tito's Taco Palace packs its burritos" (not "their").
