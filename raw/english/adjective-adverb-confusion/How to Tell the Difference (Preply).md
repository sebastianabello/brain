# Distinguishing Adverbs from Adjectives in English

## Core definition
Adjectives modify nouns and pronouns, answering "which" or "what kind" — details like size, color, or quantity. Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs, describing "how, when, where, or to what extent."

## Key examples
- Adjective: "The dog is fast" (fast describes the noun "dog")
- Adverb: "The dog runs fast" (fast describes the verb "runs")

## Formation rules

### Standard adverb formation
Most adverbs are created by adding -ly to adjectives: slow → slowly, quick → quickly, careful → carefully.

Some adverbs form with -y from nouns (per source, though these actually form adjectives): wind → windy, sun → sunny, rain → rainy.

### Irregular exceptions
Not all adverbs follow patterns and must be memorized: good → well, fast → fast.

## Critical nuance: Good vs. Well
"You can say 'good job!' and 'well done' but not 'very well weather.' The correct phrase is 'very good weather.'" — "good" functions as an adjective for nouns, while "well" functions as an adverb for actions.
