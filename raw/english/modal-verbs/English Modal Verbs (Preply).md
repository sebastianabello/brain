# English Modal Verbs
Fuente: https://preply.com/en/blog/english-modal-verbs/

## Core Modal Verbs

The article identifies ten primary modal verbs: can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to.

## Main Uses and Functions

1. **Ability** — "can + bare infinitive verb". Example: *Can you swim? Yes, I can swim.* Common error: avoid adding "to" after can.
2. **Permission** — can, may, might. Example: *May we go to the store now?* Formal requests require "may" rather than "can."
3. **Obligation and Necessity** — must, have to. Example: *Your child must have the shorts before the next game.* Structure requires bare infinitive, not gerund forms.
4. **Requests and Offers** — *Would you like a glass of water?* Formula: subject + modal + verb.
5. **Certainty** — *She should arrive at 7 o'clock.* Different modals convey varying degrees of confidence.
6. **General Truths** — "can" for present, "could" for past conditions. Example: *It can rain a lot here in November.*
7. **Predictions** — will. Example: *Technology will likely change the way we live.*
8. **Past Time Reference** — modal + have + past participle. Example: *The kids must have eaten all the pizza.*
9. **Deduction and Inference** — must and can't. Example: *She can't be over 30 – she looks so young!*
10. **Prohibition** — *You can't take weapons.* Formula: modal + not + bare infinitive.
11. **Advice and Recommendations** — should, ought to, had better. Example: *You should think about why you are learning it.*
12. **Lost Opportunities/Regret** — modal + have + past participle. Example: *I should've practiced more.*
13. **Wishes and Preferences** — would, would rather. Example: *I would like to play football.* Note: "would rather" requires bare infinitive.

## Semi-Modals

These behave like modals but are verb phrases: used to (past habits), have to / have got to (necessity), ought to (advice), need to (requirement), supposed to / meant to (intentions). Key difference: most semi-modals include "to" before the infinitive.

## Common Mistakes and Corrections

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| He can to swim | He can swim |
| Can I to help? | Can I help? |
| has to wearing | has to wear |
| My key willn't work | My key won't work |
| I ought have texted | I ought to have texted |
| I'd rather to eat | I'd rather not eat |
| might being play | might be playing |

## Key Rules

1. Bare infinitive rule: true modals never use "to" before verbs.
2. Context matters: choose modals based on whether discussing ability, permission, obligation, or possibility.
3. Tense formation: past meanings require "have + past participle" after the modal.
4. Progressive forms: use "modal + be + present participle".

## Usage Tips

Practice constructing sentences with various modals; listen to native speakers; seek feedback; distinguish formal ("may") vs informal ("can") contexts; "must" for non-negotiable requirements vs. "should" for recommendations.
